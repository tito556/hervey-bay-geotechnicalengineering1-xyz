Properties in Pialba often settle differently than those in Urangan, despite being only a few kilometers apart. The coastal sands and compressible clays that underlie Hervey Bay create stark contrasts in bearing behavior from one suburb to the next. A single house may show no cracks while a neighboring structure tilts visibly. That difference is differential settlement. We measure it by comparing layer compressibility across the site, then model the expected movement under design loads. Without this analysis, a slab designed for stiff sand may crack when it straddles a soft clay lens. Every case is unique here. We combine borelogs with consolidation testing to quantify how much each layer will shorten under load.

A 10-millimeter differential movement can crack a brick veneer wall. We detect it before concrete is poured.

Service characteristics in Hervey Bay

Much of Hervey Bay sits on Quaternary beach deposits interbedded with estuarine clays. The water table lies less than 2 meters deep near the coast. These conditions amplify differential settlement risks. We apply AS 1726:2017 for site classification and AS 2870:2011 for residential slab design. Our laboratory runs oedometer tests to determine compression index (Cc) and recompression index (Cr). We also correlate SPT N-values with constrained modulus for preliminary estimates. Shallow Settlement Analysis

Frequently asked questions

How long does a typical differential settlement analysis take in Hervey Bay?

A standard residential analysis, including sampling and oedometer testing, takes 7 to 10 business days. Larger commercial projects with multiple boreholes may require 3 to 4 weeks.

What is the difference between total settlement and differential settlement?

Total settlement is how much the entire foundation moves downward. Differential settlement is the relative movement between two points on the same structure. A 50 mm total settlement is harmless if uniform, but a 25 mm difference across a 6-meter span can crack walls and jam doors.
